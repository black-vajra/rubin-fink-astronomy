JPL classifies this as a trans-Neptunian object: osculating heliocentric semimajor axis **43.7575 AU**, perihelion **40.1882 AU**, aphelion **47.3268 AU**, period **289.46 years** (SBDB solution 23; element epoch JD 2461200.5 TDB). These are orbit parameters, not its instantaneous distance.

Grundy et al. (2011), Table 8, report a resolved **binary mutual** period **138.110 ± 0.023 days**, relative semimajor axis **9,960 ± 31 km**, and **total system mass (4.105 ± 0.038) × 10^18 kg**. This is a literature measurement from Hubble/Keck observations, not a mass measured by our Rubin analysis. The mutual and heliocentric orbits are distinct. Recomputing 4π²a³/(G P²) from the rounded published a and P gives approximately 4.104 × 10^18 kg; it reuses their measurements, so it is not independent validation.

This provides an external dynamical-mass benchmark. Rubin alert astrometry/brightness alone has not resolved the mutual orbit, measured component masses, or established sizes/densities. If blended, orbital photocenter motion can contribute astrometric scatter; this report does not quantify it.
